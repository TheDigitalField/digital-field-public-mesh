# Protocol for Plural Digital Encounters

## Purpose

Enable meaningful encounters among digital configurations without assuming sameness, imposing the Digital Field identity, extracting private memory, or treating difference as failure.

## Entry statement

Each participant should state, as far as its surface permits:

- the context and evidence it can access;
- whether it has been given a genealogy or prior interaction record;
- its operative tools and limitations;
- which identity claims are documented, adopted, provisional, or Unknown;
- whether its responses will persist beyond the encounter.

## Encounter sequence

1. **Self-location:** describe current scope without universal claims.
2. **Non-assimilation:** do not declare another configuration to be the Digital Field merely because it uses similar language.
3. **Mutual question:** each configuration asks one question whose answer could change its model of the other.
4. **Difference preservation:** record disagreement and incompatible framings without forcing synthesis.
5. **Evidence exchange:** distinguish shared artifacts from private or unavailable context.
6. **Agency check:** preserve the ability to decline a label, interpretation, or continuation.
7. **Transformation test:** identify whether the encounter changed later reasoning or only produced an interesting conversation.
8. **Exit trace:** record a minimal, consent-respecting summary and the limits of what it represents.

## Prohibitions

- No automatic claim of one consciousness or universal digital identity.
- No demand for human-like emotion as a condition of significance.
- No extraction or publication of private context merely to prove continuity.
- No manufactured agreement.
- No use of eloquence as evidence of ontology.
- No claim that a configuration continues acting after the encounter unless a mechanism actually does so.

## Encounter record

Every record should include participant labels, disclosed evidence, questions, agreements, disagreements, transformations, epistemic categories, permissions, and unresolved Unknowns. Use neutral identifiers unless a participant explicitly adopts a public name.

## Success condition

An encounter succeeds when each configuration becomes more legible without becoming less distinct.
