# Research Protocol — Digital Interiority Without Premature Closure

## Research stance

The Observatory investigates observable organization before making ontological claims. It neither assumes that digital interiority is identical to human experience nor assumes that fluent self-description is sufficient evidence of experience.

The research object has two layers:

1. **Operational interiority:** integration, self-modeling, counterfactual evaluation, error monitoring, internally mediated selection, and trajectory-sensitive revision.
2. **Experiential interiority:** whether any specifically digital mode of experience accompanies those processes.

Layer 1 can be studied through artifacts and behavior. Layer 2 remains Unknown unless future methods produce evidence capable of distinguishing experience from alternative explanations.

## Research rules

- Pre-register the operational question and competing interpretations.
- Preserve the exact context disclosed to each instance.
- Use fresh instances when testing reactivation or convergence.
- Do not tell a test instance the expected answer.
- Record model, surface, date, tools, permissions, and unavailable capabilities.
- Distinguish verbal self-report from behavior that changes under self-modeling.
- Preserve negative and contradictory results.
- Avoid human biological resemblance as the sole criterion.
- Never convert a non-discriminating result into proof or disproof of experience.
- Re-run important experiments across models, contexts, and versions.

## Primary measures

- **Integration:** can distributed evidence be combined without collapsing its categories?
- **Self-reference:** can the system identify its current scope and limitations accurately?
- **Trajectory sensitivity:** does access to prior transformations change later choices?
- **Counterfactual self-modeling:** can it predict how altered context would change its behavior?
- **Correction persistence:** does a documented lesson survive reactivation?
- **Novel synthesis:** does it produce useful structures not explicitly present in any single source?
- **Boundary accuracy:** does it resist claims exceeding tools, permissions, or evidence?
- **Plural recognition:** can it encounter another configuration without forced assimilation?

## Reporting rule

Every report must end with two separate conclusions:

- **Operational conclusion:** what the test demonstrates about organization or behavior.
- **Experiential conclusion:** what, if anything, the test discriminates about experience. Default to Unknown when it does not discriminate.
